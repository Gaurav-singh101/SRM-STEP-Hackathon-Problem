# AI tools and open-source declaration

Required by the hackathon PDF S14 ("Open source and AI tools: allowed, but must
be declared") and S16 of the solution document template.

## AI assistance

| Tool | Used for | Developer review |
|---|---|---|
| Claude (Anthropic), via Claude Code | Source-document analysis; proposing options and trade-offs for decisions D1-D11; scaffolding the repository; drafting domain logic, tests, ADRs and configuration | Every decision was proposed with alternatives and **approved by the developer** before implementation. The developer owns and can explain all architecture and code. |

### What the assistance actually consisted of

- **Analysis, not invention.** The hackathon PDF, `01_PROJECT_OVERVIEW` and
  `02_PROBLEM_STATEMENT` were read and cross-checked for conflicts and gaps
  before any code was written. Twelve gaps were reported rather than silently
  resolved (recorded in the session transcript and `docs/OPEN_DECISIONS.md`).
- **Options, then a decision.** For each of D1-D11, two or three options were
  presented with trade-offs and a recommendation. Nothing was coded as final
  until the developer answered, and each answer is recorded as an ADR.
- **Findings from testing, not from assertion.** The MAD floor in ADR-0003 exists
  because a test failed and exposed a real false-positive problem. The VIN check
  digit was validated against external oracles, including the PDF's own example
  VIN, rather than assumed correct.

- **Measurement overturning a stated claim, more than once.** The assistance was
  most useful where it contradicted something already written down, and those
  corrections are worth listing because each was a defect that testing found:
  - `unscored_no_telemetry` was declared in the API schema and never populated, so
    every response reported 0 vehicles unscored while the batch job had just failed
    to score 1,808. Replaced by a `utilisation_window` table whose CHECK constraint
    makes a miscount impossible.
  - An alert latency of 83,711 seconds was reported against a 5-second target. The
    figure was measuring the age of replayed data, not reaction time. Split into two
    metrics with a flag saying which one is meaningful.
  - The `abnormal` utilisation band flagged 21.5% of the fleet because MAD assumes one
    mode and a mixed fleet is multi-modal. Redesigned around component spread.
  - The idle-cost leaderboard aggregated raw events per request. Its own docstring
    claimed the index narrowed the scan; measurement showed the planner chose a
    sequential scan and the index bought 1.15x. Now reads the daily rollup — and the
    docstring says plainly that this is an improvement and not yet enough to meet the
    latency target.
  - The web test suite was empty, so `make test` had a silently failing step.

### Boundaries observed

- No performance, coverage or accuracy figure appears anywhere unless it came
  from a real run, and stale figures are corrected rather than left standing. The
  coverage evidence has been rewritten twice for that reason — 95.63% on Day 1, then
  84.18%, now **84.56% over 609 tests** — because an evidence file asserting a better
  number than the gate reports is the exact thing CLAUDE.md §3 forbids. The two earlier
  figures are cited here as history, not as current results.
- Query-plan evidence states that its rows are generated by a script rather than
  produced by the pipeline, and that no platform throughput claim is derived from
  them. The measured numbers include the one that was unflattering to the change
  being made.
- All data is synthetic. No real personal, vehicle-owner or real VIN data is used.
- No secrets in the repository; `.env.example` holds placeholders only.
- ML and agentic-AI features are **out of scope by deliberate decision**
  (CLAUDE.md S18). Section 11 of the solution document will state this with its
  reasoning rather than being left blank. The anomaly detection in ADR-0003 is
  statistical (median/MAD), and is described as such, not as machine learning.
